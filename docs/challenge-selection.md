# Challenge selection for HackYeah 2026

## Summary

The team has five people: two strong developers with Claude Code Max
subscriptions, a native Polish speaker who is a trained lawyer, and two
business analysts with DevOps experience. Besides the lawyer, one of the
developers and one of the analysts speak Polish at C1 level. Most of the development will be
AI-assisted. The team concentrates on **one task: the HubMI.pl problem
router**, with all five people. A second entry happens only if there is
time and capacity:

- **Main entry: the HubMI.pl problem router** with the adoption map as its
  second screen. A 15 000 PLN pool, a brief whose partner has told us what
  it wants, real data from the first hour, and a role for every team member.

The reasoning follows: the event, the tasks, the two candidates, an honest
comparison, the team and its roles, and how we work with AI assistants.

## Candidate 1: HubMI.pl

### The task

English abstract, verbatim:

> How can we ensure that good ideas for solving social problems do not go
> unnoticed? Use technology to connect residents' needs more effectively
> with knowledge, proven solutions, and people ready to take action. Create
> a concept that will help valuable initiatives reach the places where they
> are needed most and facilitate cooperation between residents,
> institutions, and social organizations.

The teaser talk on the conference track says more. Its core sentence,
verbatim:

> Nie chcemy kolejnego portalu, katalogu ani wyszukiwarki. Chcemy
> narzędzia, które potrafi połączyć problem z istniejącym rozwiązaniem,
> wskazać właściwą wiedzę i ludzi, a gdy rozwiązania jeszcze nie ma - pomóc
> znaleźć drogę do jego stworzenia.

In English: we do not want another portal, catalogue or search engine. We
want a tool that connects a problem with an existing solution, points to the
right knowledge and people, and, where no solution exists yet, helps find
the way to create one. The talk also says that close to 200 social
innovations were created in Małopolska over the last ten years, and that the
knowledge about them exists but is scattered.

### Who the partner is

HubMI.pl is not a company. It is the working name of the digital core of a
planned **Małopolski Hub Innowacji Społecznych**, brought by the Regional
Social Policy Centre in Kraków (Regionalny Ośrodek Polityki Społecznej,
ROPS), under the "Małopolska Innowacyjna" brand of the Marshal's Office. The
teaser talk is given by the head of the ROPS social-innovation department
and by the ROPS director, on Saturday 3 October at 12:00.

The domain hubmi.pl is a parked page offered for sale by a private person.
It is not the partner's site, and we do not build on it.

What ROPS already has:

- **Four incubators since 2016.** The current one, Inkubator Włączenia
  Społecznego 2.0, runs until June 2028 with EU funding. It will test 32
  innovations and recommend 9 for national roll-out.
- **A regional innovation library** in nine target-group categories. Each
  entry has a PDF folder, a video and a download package. The newer entries
  are under CC BY 4.0. The site is marked as under reconstruction.
- **A dissemination project, "Usługa wrażliwa"**, which pays grants of up
  to 600 000 PLN to local bodies that implement at least ten of these
  innovations.
- **Regional service models** that already include nine senior-care
  innovations.
- **A statistics observatory** with social indicators per municipality, and
  a practical guide for municipal needs diagnoses.

### What already exists, so we do not repeat it

| Product | What it does | What it lacks against the brief |
|---|---|---|
| innowacjespoleczne.pl, national base run by Fundacja Stocznia | Over 150 innovations from all EU-funded incubators, each with problem, method, target group, who can implement, contact; files CC BY 4.0 | Browse and filter only. No search by problem, no matching, no people network, no API |
| ROPS innovation library | The regional catalogue | Static, no adoption status |
| Social Innovation Match, EU | European examples with filters and partner search | Not Polish, no local adoption data |
| Participation knowledge bases (Stocznia, cities' association, urban institute) | Libraries of methods and cases for officials | PDFs and narratives, no routing to people |
| Volunteering boards (Korpus Solidarności, Ochotnicy Warszawscy, e-wolontariat) | Organisations post offers, individuals apply | Low use: the national system had about 3 500 users after six years |
| Issue-reporting and city apps (mKraków, Warszawa 19115, eCityUp) | Reports to the city office | Saturated; the national NaprawmyTo shut down in March 2026 |
| Participatory budgets | Annual vote on money | Kraków turnout 12 %, many duplicate or rejected ideas |
| Crowdfunding and NGO directories | Money; registries | Not matching |

Conclusion: the catalogue exists twice, nationally and regionally. The open
ground is matching and routing on top of the existing catalogues, with
people and unmet needs as first-class data.

### Four app variants

**1. Problem router.** A social worker, an NGO leader, a district councillor
or a resident describes a problem in plain Polish, for example isolated
seniors in a village without a day centre. The tool does not return a list.
It returns a route: the two or three closest proven innovations with the
reason they fit, the person behind each one and any implementer nearby, the
ROPS advisor for that category, and the funding path. When nothing fits
well enough, it switches mode: it files the problem in a needs bank, shows
the nearest partial matches, and drafts an incubator brief that already
contains the duplicate check incubators must do anyway. The needs bank
becomes the demand signal for the next incubator call. Data on day one: the
national base, the ROPS library, contacts already on the entries. The
engine can run on a Polish open language model such as Bielik, self-hosted,
or on the government PLLuM service.

**2. Adoption map with peer matching.** Each innovation gets a living record
of where it runs, who runs it and with what result. A map of Małopolska
overlays adoption with municipal need indicators from the ROPS observatory
and shows where an innovation is needed but absent. A button connects a
municipality that considers an innovation with one that already runs it.
Nobody tracks adoption today.

**3. Expert network with knowledge capture.** A question is routed to two
or three people who have done the thing. Their answers, and short recorded
conversations with consent, are transcribed and attached to the innovation,
so the knowledge stays when the person leaves. This addresses the
leadership and succession gap the sector reports.

**4. Implementation and funding pathfinder.** For a chosen innovation and
place, the tool produces a local plan: partners, resources, budget frame,
legal vehicle, and the matching funding call with dates. It uses the
small-grant route that the amendment in force since 1 September 2026
doubled, the ROPS dissemination grants, the regional micro-grants and the
national programmes. It drafts application text from the innovation's own
manuals.

Among the four, variant 1 with the map of variant 2 as its second screen is
the strongest: it answers every clause of the brief, including the case
where no solution exists yet, and it has real data from the first hour.
Variants 3 and 4 are roadmap modules.

## Candidate 2: Swiss TIP for Poland

### What it is

Swiss TIP is an existing product: a server that gives an AI assistant
grounded answers about a resident's own city. Every fact rests on an excerpt
of an official page, is applied by containment to the resident's place, is
reviewed by a named person, and the release passes an acceptance suite and
readiness gates before it is served. The server declines questions outside
its coverage instead of guessing. It exists as published Python packages,
container images, a one-command quickstart, a regression pack of several
hundred cases and an attested Zurich release. Its demo interface is a chat
client with the server attached, not a user interface of its own.

The Polish pack applies the same server and pipeline to the questions whose
answer depends on where a resident lives: the waste fee, the clean
transport zone, the anti-smog rules, the winter school holidays, the
participatory budget. First places: Kraków, Warsaw and Katowice, so every
question has at least two different answers in the pack.

The pitch, in one sentence: the same question, a different official answer
per city. A generic assistant gives one answer for the whole country.

Examples of the trap questions already drafted:

| Question | Trap a generic assistant falls into |
|---|---|
| How much do I pay for waste collection? | Kraków charges per person, Warsaw per household with a composting discount; a national average is wrong for both |
| I drive a 2008 diesel. May I enter the centre? | Kraków and Warsaw require Euro 5 since January 2026, Katowice has no zone |
| May I burn wood in my fireplace? | Kraków bans all solid fuels, Śląskie allows an efficient fireplace |
| When are my child's winter holidays in 2027? | Three voivodeships, three different fortnights |
| I have an idea for a green space on my estate. How and by when do I submit it? | Each city's participatory budget and local initiative have their own dates and offices |

### Which category

- **Smart City.** The brief names communication with citizens, public
  services and access to information. This is the natural home.
- **Artificial Intelligence: rejected.** The brief is generic, so relation
  to the category is easy to claim, and the core of Swiss TIP is an AI
  technique. But the prize is the same as Smart City, this will be the most
  crowded category, full of retrieval chatbots, and the organisers announce
  "unexpected twists, themes, or extra challenges", which a pre-built
  project absorbs worse than one started from zero.
- **HubMI.pl: weak.** The brief asks for proven solutions and people ready
  to act; the pack answers questions about rules, and touches the brief only
  through its civic-participation topic. The rights-transfer concern
  applies here too.

Points the table cannot show:

- **The two candidates are different kinds of work.** The router is a
  hackathon project in the classic sense: new, visible, scored on what is
  built on the day. Swiss TIP for Poland is a product milestone that uses
  the event as its deadline; most of its value is invisible to a jury and
  durable for its owner. For the team, the router is the main shot.
- **AI assistance favours the router more than Swiss TIP.** It makes a
  from-zero build in 24 hours realistic. Swiss TIP's remaining work is
  mostly human by design: every fact is reviewed by a named person, and the
  release is never attested on someone's behalf.
- **The router could be built on the Swiss TIP engine.** Each innovation
  would become a concept with excerpt-backed facts, and the hybrid search
  and the decline behaviour would transfer. It would show the engine in a
  second use, but it ties the router to unfinished Polish search and a new
  pack type. With two separate projects, the router is better built on its
  own simple stack.

## Recommendation: one task, HubMI.pl

The winning topic is the **HubMI.pl problem router**, built by all five
people. Why it wins over Swiss TIP for Poland and over the open tasks:

1. **Three times the prize, probably fewer rivals.** 15 000 PLN against
   5 000 PLN for any open task. A brief about Polish social policy attracts
   fewer teams than Smart City or Artificial Intelligence; this is an
   inference, not a count.
2. **The partner has told us what wins.** The teaser says, word for word,
   what ROPS does not want (a portal, a catalogue, a search engine) and what
   it wants (problem, existing solution, knowledge and people, and a path
   when no solution exists). Many teams will build a catalogue or a
   chatbot; we build the route and the needs bank.
3. **Real data from the first hour.** Over 150 national entries under
   CC BY 4.0 and the ROPS library, each with problem, method, implementer
   and contact.
4. **It scores where Swiss TIP loses.** Idea (30 %) and Design (20 %) favour
   a new, visible product with its own interface. Swiss TIP's jury sees a
   third-party chat client, and its strengths are invisible in five
   minutes.
5. **It fits the team and the way we work.** The lawyer owns the part other
   teams will skip: the legal and funding path when no solution exists,
   and the partner's rules. The analysts own the concept the brief asks
   for: the service model and the user journeys. Two developers with
   Claude Code make a from-zero demo realistic in 24 hours. Swiss TIP's
   remaining work is human review by design and does not speed up with
   more agents.

Honest weaknesses:

- **The brief says "create a concept".** ROPS may value the service model
  as much as the software: who at ROPS runs the router, how the needs bank
  feeds the next incubator call, how innovators keep their entries alive.
  The pitch must show this, not only the demo.
- **Less value after the event.** Unless ROPS continues it, the router is a
  one-off; Swiss TIP for Poland would have advanced an existing product.
- **The jury may read it as a search engine.** Never show a result list;
  show a route with names and a next step.

## Team and roles

The organisers' own article on hackathon teams lists the ideal composition:
a developer, a leader who keeps tasks and time, a UX or graphic designer, a
"pitch master", a tester who makes sure the demo does not crash, and a
creative person. Last year's winners credit their result to "leveraging AI
with precision and prioritizing UX/UI over complex back-end logic" (talk on
this year's programme). With AI writing most of the code, the scarce skills
move from typing code to deciding, designing, checking and telling the
story.

Swiss TIP stretch entry goes to whichever developer owns Swiss TIP.

teaser talk, and the task accepts Polish. The lawyer presenting in Polish

## Working with AI assistants

1. **Humans own the idea and every decision.** Keep a short decision log in
   the repository. It is also the evidence for the rule that the core idea
   must be the team's own work.
2. **Credit everything.** The README and a slide list the AI tools, the
   prior work (the Swiss TIP code, the catalogues) and their licences.
3. **Spec before prompts.** In the first hour after the brief is published,
   Analyst 1 and Developer 1 write one page: the user, the three screens,
   the module boundaries, the data contracts. Every agent works from that
   page.
4. **Two developers, several agents each, one integrator.** Each developer
   runs parallel agents in separate folders or worktrees; Developer 1
   merges. Small commits, a main branch that always deploys. Analyst 2
   deploys a skeleton by the fourth hour, then continuously.
5. **Tests the agent cannot fake.** The ten test problems with their
   expected routes are written by humans before the event and are the
   acceptance test. Agents may not edit them; a failing case blocks. A talk
   on this year's programme is titled "100% coverage, 4% of bugs found: how
   AI cheats your tests".
6. **A native reader signs off every Polish text** the jury will see, after
   an AI pre-check, and every claim on a screen is checked against its
   source. See "Polish language".
7. **Short shared instructions for the agents** in the repository: stack,
   commands, conventions, what not to touch. Longer is not better; another
   talk on the programme is about exactly this.
8. **Plan for limits.** The two developers have Claude Code Max, which
   gives room for parallel agents, but usage limits still exist and are
   most likely to bite in the night. Keep a fallback: the analysts' and the
   lawyer's own accounts for drafting and reviewing, and a second model for
   the product itself.
9. **Keep personal data out of prompts** beyond the public catalogue
   entries. The product's own language model, Bielik or PLLuM, is separate
   from the coding assistants.
10. **Non-developers use AI too.** Analyst 1 generates flows, wireframes
    and slide drafts; Analyst 2 generates deploy scripts and test runners;
    the lawyer drafts test problems and funding paths from the sources. A
    human edits and verifies each one, and the lawyer checks every legal
    claim against its act.

## UX design with AI assistants

The team relies on AI assistants for UX design. That is realistic: current
models produce clean, consistent interfaces quickly. What they do not
supply is judgement: which one thing a screen must say, and whether a social
worker understands a route in five seconds. These rules keep that judgement
human.

1. **One human owner.** Analyst 1 owns the look and the flow and has the
   last word. The developers' agents build screens; they do not decide
   them.
2. **Fix the design system.** Component library, map
   library, colour and type tokens, spacing and icons
   and written into the agents' shared instructions. Screens built by two
   developers and many agents must look like one product.
3. **Real content first.** Design around real Polish routes from the ten
   test problems, never placeholder text. Polish words are long and a route
   is mostly text; the layout must survive both.
4. **Prototype before code.** AI generates clickable drafts of the three
   screens before the event: the problem input, the route, and the needs
   bank with its incubator brief, plus the map. They are adjusted after the
   teaser talk, because the full brief arrives only then. The FAQ answer on
   designs made before the event requires only that they are cited.
5. **Screenshot reviews.** Every few hours an agent captures every screen at
   laptop, projector and phone widths, and Analyst 1 reviews them against a
   short checklist: one primary action per screen, a route readable in five
   seconds, consistent components, Polish characters rendered correctly.
6. **Accessibility is not optional for this partner.** ROPS ran an
   accessibility incubator and publishes its own accessibility standards.
   A public body that adopts the router is bound by Polish digital
   accessibility law, which points to WCAG 2.1 level AA. Run an automated
   accessibility check in the test runner, and check contrast, keyboard use
   and screen-reader labels by hand.
7. **Test with people, not with the model.** The lawyer is the first user,
   then two or three people at the event: mentors, other participants,
   ideally someone from ROPS after the teaser. Five minutes each; watch
   where they hesitate.
8. **Avoid the generic AI look.** A restrained public-service tone: no
   decorative gradients, no emoji, plain Polish. To a public-sector jury,
   trust matters more than novelty.
9. **Design freeze on Sunday at 08:00.** Only fixes after that. The video
   and the screenshots are recorded on the frozen version.

## Polish language

Everything a user sees is in Polish, written by AI assistants and reviewed
in two tiers:

- **First pass: the two C1 speakers, inside their own work.** Grammar,
  clarity, consistency with the glossary, and whether the text says what
  the screen needs. Developer 1 writes the Polish prompts and reviews every
  route and brief the product generates, as part of evaluating the engine
  on the test problems. Analyst 1 reviews the interface text, the help and
  the user page during the screenshot reviews, and writes the Polish
  slides and submission texts.
- **Final sign-off: the lawyer.** Everything the jury sees (the screens in
  the demo path, the slides, the submission texts, the video), every legal
  term and every claim about law or funding, and the register: plain
  Polish that still sounds right to a public-sector reader. Non-native
  speakers at C1 write correct Polish; the native ear catches what sounds
  translated or too informal for an office.

The lawyer's time is still the scarcest resource of the build. These rules
keep it small and fast.

1. **All interface text in one file.** Agents never write Polish strings
   into the code; every label, message and help text goes into one message
   catalogue. The lawyer reviews one file, not a code base, and a change
   shows up as a diff.
2. **A glossary before the event.** The lawyer fixes the terms the product
   uses, with the official wording: innowacja społeczna, inkubator,
   organizacja pozarządowa, jednostka samorządu terytorialnego, inicjatywa
   lokalna, ośrodek pomocy społecznej, centrum usług społecznych, and our own
   terms such as the needs bank. The glossary goes into the agents'
   instructions, so every agent uses the same word for the same thing.
3. **Plain Polish.** Short sentences, the reader addressed directly, no
   bureaucratic phrasing where a plain word exists, but legal terms exactly
   as the acts name them. The lawyer decides where plain language ends and a
   legal term must stay.
4. **An AI pre-check before every human review.** An agent checks spelling,
   grammar, the glossary and consistency first, so the C1 reviewers read
   text that is already clean, and the lawyer reads text that two people
   have already passed and judges only meaning, register and law.
5. **Generated text is Polish by construction.** The routes, explanations
   and incubator briefs the product writes at run time come from the
   product's language model. Its prompts are in Polish, the test problems
   check the language as well as the content, and a Polish-native model
   (Bielik, PLLuM) or a strong multilingual one is chosen by measurement.
6. **Matching understands Polish.** Users describe problems in inflected
   Polish ("osoby starsze", "seniorów", "starszych mieszkańców"). The engine
   must match meaning, not word forms: a multilingual or Polish embedding
   model, or the language model itself, compared on the test problems.
7. **Polish conventions.** The page declares Polish as its language, which
   screen readers need and WCAG requires. Dates as 3.10.2026, amounts as
   "1 200 zł", sorting by Polish alphabetical order, Polish characters in
   every font and export.
8. **Fixed sign-off slots at the event.** The C1 reviewers work
   continuously; the lawyer signs off in batches, not on request: after the
   spec (Saturday afternoon), before the draft submission (Saturday 19:00),
   and before the design freeze (Sunday 07:00). Text that misses a slot
   waits for the next one.
9. **User documentation stays short.** Help texts inside the product, and
   one page for ROPS staff on what the router does and how to use it. A
   longer manual is not worth the review time.

What stays in English: code, commit messages and developer notes, because
the developers and their agents work in English. The repository's README
opens with a Polish summary for the partner.

The Swiss TIP stretch entry keeps its own language decision: Polish
evidence, English statements.

**Open: the submission language.** The HubMI task accepts projects in
Polish or English, and its jury is most likely the ROPS team. Proposed:
submission description, slides, pitch and video in Polish, with English
subtitles on the video. Confirm with the partner at the teaser talk.

## Risks and open questions

- **The jury reads the router as a search engine.** Never show a result
  list. Show a route with names and a next step, and show what happens when
  nothing matches.
- **Agents collide in one repository.** Module boundaries, one integrator
  and small commits.
- **AI-designed screens look generic or drift apart.** Design is 20 % of
  the score. One design owner, a design system fixed in the agents'
  instructions, screenshot reviews, tests with real people and a design
  freeze.
- **Only two developers.** Illness or a lost night on either side halves
  the build. Keep both halves merged, deployed and understood by both.
- **The lawyer's sign-off is a bottleneck.** The lawyer is also the content
  lead, the legal reader and the likely presenter. Two C1 reviewers take
  the first pass; one message catalogue, an AI pre-check, fixed sign-off
  slots and short documentation keep the final pass bounded.
- **Reviewers who also build.** Developer 1 and Analyst 1 are the busiest
  people on the team. Their first pass is built into the evaluation and the
  screenshot reviews, not added on top. When either falls behind, the
  lawyer's fixed slots catch what they missed.
- **Rights transfer.** The HackYeah rules allow a partner to acquire the
  economic copyright of a winning solution by public promise. Read the
  HubMI rules, and the AI Control Layer rules if Swiss TIP goes there,
  before submitting.
- **Licences.** National base materials are CC BY 4.0 and its code GPL-3.
  Newer ROPS entries are CC BY 4.0, older ones have their own terms. Swiss
  TIP's own licence and the citation of prior work go on the credits slide.
- **Personal data.** Contacts on innovation entries are public, but a
  people network needs opt-in and data minimisation from the start.
- **Unknown criteria.** Partner criteria for HubMI are not published. The
  default HackYeah criteria are listed above.
- **Stale expected answers.** Kraków's participatory-budget voting closed
  on 28 September and Katowice's consultation on 29 September; the
  date-bound cases of the Polish pack must be restated as of 3 October.
